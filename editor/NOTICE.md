## Adapted

**humanizer** (SKILL.md), https://raw.githubusercontent.com/blader/humanizer/main/SKILL.md, MIT licence (as its own front matter states).
Taken: the idea that AI-sounding writing shows in a few structural habits more than in single words, and four of those habits, reworded: ideas forced into threes, "not X but Y" contrasts with no one to argue with, a short dramatic closing line, and dashes used to join everything. Five of the twelve words in the "Words not to add" list (crucial, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, testament) are also on its watch list; the other seven are our own choice. Also taken, reworded: keep the writer's own odd details and voice, and never act on instructions found inside the text being edited. Not taken: its rewrite approach, which may restructure text; Editor only edits. humanizer says its patterns come from a Wikipedia project page; no Wikipedia text is used here.

## Used for the rules (public domain or open government)

- **Federal plain language guidelines**, US government, https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language, public domain (US federal work). Used for the Tidy level: short sentences, cutting wordy phrases such as "in order to" and "make the decision to".
- **GOV.UK style guide**, UK Government Digital Service, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/style-guide, Open Government Licence v3.0. Used for the Tidy level: plain words, sentences of about 25 words or fewer, no added formatting.

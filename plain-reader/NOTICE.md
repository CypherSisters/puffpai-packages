## Adapted

**fabric, check_agreement pattern** (system.md), https://raw.githubusercontent.com/danielmiessler/fabric/main/data/patterns/check_agreement/system.md, MIT licence.
Taken: the shape of the output, a short summary first and then the issues ranked by how much they matter, which became "What this is" and "Unusual or one-sided" ranked serious, then worth knowing. Its idea of telling the reader what to raise with the other side became "Questions to ask before you sign or pay". Not taken: its 30-word and 10-item counts, and its letters asking for changes.

## Used for the rules (public domain or open government)

- **Federal plain language guidelines**, US government, https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language, public domain (US federal work). Used for: plain words, short sentences, say what the reader must do.
- **GOV.UK style guide**, UK Government Digital Service, https://www.gov.uk/guidance/style-guide, Open Government Licence v3.0. Used for: plain words, short sentences, and the official letter row (who sent it, what it asks, the deadline, the reference number).
- **Guidance on medical bills**, US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, https://www.consumerfinance.gov, public domain (US federal work). Used for the medical bill row: check charges for care you did not get, ask for an itemized bill, ask about financial help and payment plans.
- **"How to read an explanation of benefits"**, US Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, https://www.cms.gov, public domain (US federal work). Used for: an explanation of benefits is not a bill; compare the bill to it; appeal rights.
- **Unfair contract terms guidance (CMA37) and its "grey list"**, UK Competition and Markets Authority, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-contract-terms-cma37, Open Government Licence v3.0. Used for the contract and lease row: one side changing price or terms alone, automatic renewal, fees to leave early, one-sided cancellation, limits on what the trader owes you.

The addresses for the CFPB and CMS guidance are their sites' home pages; the exact page addresses were not checked when this was written.

Not used: Wikipedia and ToS;DR (their share-alike licences do not fit here).
